# Lina Olivetree Quick Brand Reference

## Brand Essence
Calm, literary, nature-rooted illustrated products with a clear reforestation promise, expressed through tactile paper-inspired design and a personal studio voice.

## Approved Tagline
Stories, paper, and planted futures

## Approved Give-Back Promise
Every 5 items sold helps fund the planting of 1 tree in Brazil.

## Personality
- Warm
- Reflective
- Artistic
- Grounded
- Honest

## Voice
- Gentle, personal, and quietly optimistic
- Poetic but clear
- Never hype-driven

## Fonts
- Headings: `Cormorant Garamond`
- Body and UI: `Inter`
- Handwritten accent: `Shadows Into Light`

Rule:
If only two fonts are used, use `Cormorant Garamond` and `Inter`. If all three are used, keep `Shadows Into Light` as an accent for selected labels, notes, or emphasis rather than dense interface text.

## Color Palette
Primary:
- Olive `#5A5A40`
- Cream `#F5F2ED`
- Terracotta `#C05C3C`
- Paper White `#FFFFFF`

Supporting:
- Olive Light `#8B8B6B`
- Terracotta Light `#D98B72`
- Bark `#3F3A2F`
- Moss `#727557`

Rule:
Most brand work should rely mainly on the 4 primary colors.
Soft use of supporting colors is welcome when it helps create depth, paper warmth, or a handmade studio mood.

## Imagery
- Soft natural light
- Paper, books, pencils, sketches, botanical details
- Warm neutrals, muted greens, tactile textures
- Intimate, cozy, and personal, never glossy, corporate, or generic
- Handmade paper cues, notebook details, studio-table composition, and visible process are on-brand
- Slight imperfection is welcome when it adds warmth, humanity, and studio character

## Hero Shot
A sunlit handmade still life with one hero product on warm paper, linen, or a working studio surface, surrounded by subtle botanical elements, sketch tools, paper scraps, or notebook details. The product stays central; the mood should suggest story, process, and care rather than a polished magazine spread.

## Visual Language
- Blend literary calm with tactile stationery cues
- Use paper textures, soft borders, lightly imperfect frames, visible layers, and scrapbook details carefully
- Let the interface feel handmade, personal, and crafted, but not messy
- Keep decorative motifs supportive of the artwork, not louder than it
- Favor warmth, texture, and charm over polished editorial restraint

## Journal Design Tokens (Markdown Extensions)
To maintain the "Sketchbook" aesthetic, two custom triggers are used in journal content:

- `|` **Literary Quote**: Renders a sophisticated quote with a Terracotta left border (`Cormorant Garamond` font).
- `>` **Studio Note**: Renders a tactile yellow Post-it with Terracotta Washi Tape and handwritten text (`Shadows Into Light` font).

Rule: Use `|` for book citations or poetic breaks, and `>` for intimate asides, sketches-in-progress notes, or personal reminders.

## Always
- Keep the brand personal and specific
- Pair beauty with clarity
- Make the planting promise visible
- Use tactile, hand-made visual cues
- Allow moments of studio warmth and paper-craft character
- Let the work feel human, approachable, and a little imperfect

## Never
- Overstate impact
- Use urgency-heavy sales language
- Sound corporate or trend-chasing
- Let props or sustainability messaging overpower the art
- Let decorative scrapbook elements become so dominant that the work feels kitschy or childish
